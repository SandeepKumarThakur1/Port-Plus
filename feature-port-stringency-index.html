Port Stringency Index

How strict is Port State Control at your destination port?

Same ship. Two ports. Different results.

Imagine the same ship facing a Port State Control inspection at two different ports. At the first port, it receives 2 deficiencies. At the second, it receives 8.

The ship is the same. The crew is the same. The maintenance standards are the same. So why such a difference?

Part of the answer could be the port itself. Some ports tend to be stricter than others.

One inspection alone cannot tell us much. But when we look at tens of thousands of inspections, patterns begin to emerge.

That is what the Port Stringency Index measures. It helps you understand how strict Port State Control tends to be at your destination port.

Comparing ports fairly takes more than averages.

Comparing the strictness of two ports is not as simple as comparing their average deficiencies or Port State Control detention rates.

Consider a port that regularly receives older bulk carriers and another that mainly handles newer vessels. The first port may record more deficiencies, but that doesn't necessarily mean its PSC inspections are stricter. The difference could simply be due to the types of ships being inspected.

To account for this, we analyse years of PSC inspection records using machine learning. Our model compares vessels with similar characteristics and examines how these comparable ships perform across different ports, including those covered by the Paris MoU and Tokyo MoU.

By accounting for differences in the vessels visiting each port, the model estimates how strict each port tends to be, independently of the general condition and characteristics of its visiting fleet.

The result is a Port Stringency Index that goes beyond traditional deficiency averages and detention rates, giving you a more meaningful picture of the PSC inspection environment at your destination port.
